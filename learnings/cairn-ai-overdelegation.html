Cairn — When AI Builds More Than I Understand

How delegating too much to AI led to scope drift, lost project visibility, and lessons about owning product decisions.

Project: Cairn · Outcome: Discontinued

What I wanted

Build persistent, project-aware memory for AI coding agents. Help agents continue work between sessions without losing useful decisions and context.

What happened

I increasingly delegated product planning, architecture, implementation, and documentation to AI. AI generated extensive documentation and implementation changes. I stopped reviewing everything carefully and began trusting AI summaries without understanding all decisions.

Project direction gradually became unclear. Scope expanded, architecture grew complicated, and I lost track of whether we were still solving the original problem.

What I found

Passing tests and CI did not establish useful, reliable memory retrieval. Cairn PR #66 encountered semantic-quality failures: every tested completeness candidate failed semantic qualification, and source integrity is not semantic completeness. Later changes did not complete final product qualification.

Technical implementation progress and product success are different things.

Why I stopped

Continuing required more development time and AI tokens, without clear evidence that the product was worth further investment.

Existing open-source memory solutions may already cover practical needs. Evaluate them before building another system.

I chose to stop rather than let past investment justify more work without clear value.

AI should help build the product, not silently decide what the product becomes.

What I learned

  1. Define the problem, goals, boundaries, and success criteria myself.

  2. AI should advise and execute bounded tasks, not silently own product decisions.

  3. Read and understand documentation instead of measuring progress by document count.

  4. Build a small MVP and validate actual usefulness.

  5. Require explanation and approval before expanding scope or architecture.

  6. Compare existing solutions before writing custom software.

  7. Stop experiments when expected learning or value no longer justifies cost.

What changes next

  • Define product direction and success criteria before coding.
  • Let AI advise and execute bounded work; require approval for scope changes.
  • Test the smallest useful solution and evaluate existing tools before expanding.

Evidence and references
